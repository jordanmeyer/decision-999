# Other media

Read only the portion relevant to the task. Shared mark, palette, type and imagery guidance still applies; do not invent a comprehensive medium standard where evidence is unavailable.

## Email and social graphics

**Duke recommendation:** Georgia is an official option where web-font support is limited. **Skill defaults:** use supported fonts/fallbacks, readable short hierarchy, and platform-appropriate dimensions. Inspect actual email-client or platform crops rather than assuming the source file matches delivery. **General accessibility practice:** supply meaningful alternative text and include important graphic text in accessible message copy; check captioning and contrast. Do not use a graphic as the only location of an event date or registration instruction.

## Video and motion

The Branding page links a video strategy guide and a social-video graphics package. Both old URLs redirect to the Communications homepage; the current Multimedia Guide is sign-in restricted. See [S14](sources.md) for exact resources. Their titles do not supply lower-third, intro/outro, timing or animation rules. Use an authorized supplied package and its instructions for package-specific work; otherwise apply shared guidance and label additional timing/layout choices as defaults. Keep official marks unaltered. General accessibility includes captions, relevant audio description and motion controls; consult [web](web.md) for the scoped Duke homepage caption requirement.

## Apparel and promotional products

**Duke rules — [Internal Request Process](https://trademarklicensing.duke.edu/InternalRequestProcess.html):** buy Duke-branded items only from Duke licensees. A generally approved vendor is not necessarily licensed. The licensee submits the design to the Office of Trademark Licensing; production follows final approval. See [S9](sources.md). Do not invent permission for a student group, a logo variation or a partner mark.

**Skill defaults:** design within the licensed vendor's actual imprint area, file requirements and material limitations. Use published print colors and obtain the appropriate authorized mark variant. Do not improvise embroidery simplifications or transfer screen-pixel dimensions to manufacturing. Show a reviewable concept and flag production dependencies. Manufacturing, purchasing and contacting vendors require scope/authorization beyond creating a design.
