      Method, data sources and limits

      THE METHOD & ITS LIMITS

      Transparent enough to disagree with.

      Fixed anchors. Visible tradeoffs.

      Each measure becomes a score from 0 to 100 using the fixed anchors below. Values beyond the endpoints are capped; changing a gate or hiding a table row never rescales the market. Weights normalize by their total. Exact score ties sort alphabetically.

      Classroom scoring anchors, fixed for every market
      Measure0 points100 points
      Revenue / year$2 million$12 million
      Growth / year0%12%
      Delivery / order$12$4
      Competition index1000

      Missing is not zero.

      Louisiana has no assumed growth value, so it stays unranked even when growth has zero weight. A delivery or setup value above its ceiling excludes a market; equality qualifies. All-zero weights give no ranking. There may be fewer than three qualifying markets.

      Separate geography from the fictional business.

      All revenue, growth, delivery, competition and setup figures are invented for this exercise. Revenue is an assumed total addressable annual market, not this company's expected sales. The competition index is an invented comparative scale. This screen omits actual customer discovery, route design, acquisition cost and validation.

      The local map uses generalized January 1, 2024 state geometry from the U.S. Census Bureau. It is suited to a state-level illustration, not legal boundaries or address selection. This twelve-state footprint is a classroom choice. Map area never enters the score. No data is uploaded or saved.
