# Documents and print

Use shared brand guidance and the artifact's existing format. **Duke recommendation:** wordmarks belong on stationery, business cards and letterhead. [Downloads & Print Resources](https://communications.duke.edu/communicator-resources/downloads-print-resources/) links Word letterhead templates (sign-in required) and an established stationery/business-card service. See [S13](sources.md). Prefer a supplied authorized template for those formal uses; do not manufacture an “official letterhead” claim.

**Duke rules:** For printed wordmarks, keep the capital D at least 3/8 inch high and the mark at least 1/2 inch from the page edge/top, in addition to its proportional buffer. Preserve official artwork. These dimensions are not a general page-margin prescription. See [marks](marks.md).

**Skill defaults:** Match typography to reading length and final size. Use semantic heading styles, comfortable prose measures and clear tables; avoid display serifs for small labels. Let content determine page count. Keep figures and captions together, check page breaks, widows/orphans, running elements and reference readability. A report, flyer and letter need different compositions.

Use published [PMS/CMYK values](color.md) for print workflows and HEX/RGB for screen. Do not calculate a replacement print formula from HEX. Specify coated/uncoated stock and production profile with the printer where needed. Shackleford's published coated PMS value needs confirmation for spot-color production; select another approved color when that dependency is unnecessary. A screen preview is not a press proof.

**General accessibility practice:** Preserve selectable text, document language, heading structure, reading order, meaningful alternatives and table headers; check the exported PDF's tags when an accessible PDF is required. Use non-color cues in charts and ensure links are descriptive.

Inspect every rendered page at final size and in an overview. Check crop, text wrapping, resolution, margins and logo measurements after export. Record separately what was checked in source, rendered pages, accessibility structure and physical print proof. A PDF export alone does not prove any of these.

If production procurement is in scope, consult the linked Duke resources for current print purchasing requirements; this skill does not order goods, choose vendors, or publish on the user's behalf.
