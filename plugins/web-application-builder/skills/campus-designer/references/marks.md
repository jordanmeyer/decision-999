# Marks and institutional scope

Sources: [S3 Logos, S4 Branding, S9 Trademark & Licensing](sources.md). A lockup is a fixed composition of a mark and other identity elements; a sub-brand identifies a Duke unit.

## Before using a mark

**Duke rule:** The linked [Trademark & Licensing policy](https://communications.duke.edu/work-with-us/policies/trademark-licensing/) requires prior Office of Trademark Licensing approval for trademark use by internal and external parties. A downloadable file, Duke affiliation, or this skill is not evidence of approval. Use authorization already established in the project; when absent, identify the specific outstanding use question. Continue content and layout work with a clearly labeled asset placeholder rather than inventing a mark or claiming a finished approved identity. Do not contact anyone without user authorization.

Academic/administrative material must not use the Iron Duke D athletics identity. The seal is for official documents and needs University Secretary approval. The emblem is restricted to the trustees, president's office and special ceremonies, with other uses requiring trustee approval. The shield is a legacy mark retired from the modern brand in 2009. Student affiliation does not automatically authorize athletics marks. Duke Health and individual schools may have distinct rules: obtain the applicable unit guide and approved files before substituting a university identity for theirs. The [Communicator Resources directory](https://communications.duke.edu/communicator-resources/) links those separate guides.

## University wordmark

**Duke rules — [Logos](https://brand.duke.edu/logos/):**

- Use an official file, either “Duke” or the version with “University”; never recreate it in Garamond, EB Garamond, an SVG trace or generated imagery.
- Preserve its aspect ratio and artwork. Do not rotate, render in 3D, alter, or add copy/images/elements without approval. Do not surround it with, or put it atop, a pattern or design.
- Keep an empty buffer on every side equal to half the capital D's height. Measure the artwork, not the file's transparent bounds. The [official spacing illustration](figures/wordmark-spacing.png) shows both versions.
- **Print only:** at least 1/2 inch from page edge/top; capital D at least 3/8 inch high. The image explicitly ties the latter to print. No official pixel minimum was found; do not convert these into a web-logo rule.

**Duke recommendation:** use the wordmark on stationery, business cards and letterhead. Other print and digital uses are permitted subject to applicable authorization.

**Skill default:** put the supplied appropriate-color asset on a plain contrasting field. Do not recolor or invert an asset to manufacture a missing variant, extract a logo from a reference diagram, or use screenshot artwork in production. For screen layouts, size for legibility and preserve the proportional buffer; inspect at narrow widths.

## Unit sub-brands

**Duke recommendations — [Branding](https://brand.duke.edu/branding/):** start with the Duke wordmark and a separator; horizontal and stacked variants are shown. General text specifies Open Sans Bold for nouns and Regular Italic for articles/prepositions. Symbols are possible; stacked unit names can slightly exceed the wordmark width.

**Evidence qualification:** The same page's [Canvas B diagram](figures/subbrand-canvas-b.png) explicitly shows Open Sans semibold, initial capitals, normal tracking, with a separator. Its six examples also include symbol and separator-free variants. The [two-line diagram on Logos](figures/subbrand-two-line.png) shows uppercase bold nouns and italic connecting words. These are distinct supplied compositions, not a license to mix their dimensions or a universal requirement to uppercase all names.

Use the approved unit asset/template as the composition authority. When creating an authorized new sub-brand, choose a specific official template and preserve its typography, alignment, separator and spacing; inspect that template's diagram instead of estimating. The linked downloadable sheets/templates were sign-in restricted during preparation. If their unavailable details are essential for a new mark, request an authorized file or institutional clarification and hold that mark work. Existing approved marks and ordinary text layouts can proceed. The public examples are enough to select a direction, not certify a newly constructed mark.

## Multiple identities

**Duke rules — same-design co-branding:** When the university wordmark appears alongside other Duke marks, do not combine them into a new lockup; follow official size, color and placement standards. This is separate from an approved unit sub-brand.

**Duke recommendations:** The wordmark should appear on the same plane as the other Duke marks and should never be larger than them. Follow these as the normal design choice while preserving their recommendation status.

**Duke instruction — external partners:** Obtain Office of Trademark Licensing guidance for dual branding with other institutions or companies. Do not infer a sponsor relationship, relative prominence, or permission from the internal co-branding examples. Content may identify verified participants without fabricating a joint logo.
