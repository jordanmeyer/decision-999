# Identity, audience, and composition

**Duke recommendation — [Identity](https://brand.duke.edu/duke-identity/):** Frame stories through five priorities: supporting exceptional thinkers; changing teaching and learning; strengthening campus life; purposeful community partnerships; and engaging Duke's global network. Values are respect, trust, inclusion, discovery, and excellence. The tone vocabulary emphasizes confidence, curiosity, boldness, community, purpose and pride. Messaging supplies the story's underlying emphasis rather than reusable promotional copy.

Duke's identity page permits creative expression while preserving the mark. Its examples include research, teaching, campus life, community service, global engagement, and architectural motifs. These examples do not mandate a Chapel photograph, arches, an institutional slogan, or a particular page layout. [Sources S2, S10](sources.md).

## Apply to the audience

**Skill defaults derived from those themes, not Duke-prescribed audience formulas:**

| Audience or purpose | Copy and presentation choice |
| --- | --- |
| Event participants | Lead with the event's value, date/location and a clear registration action. Use a concise, inviting voice. |
| Research readers | Explain the question, method, evidence and significance; use descriptive headings and substantial reading space. Confidence must match the strength of the evidence. |
| Students and prospective members | Lead with belonging, activities, practical participation details and authentic community imagery. Identify the organization accurately. |
| Community partners | Explain shared work and credit collaborators; avoid implying Duke alone caused a result. |
| Alumni and supporters | Connect participation to a documented outcome; do not invent impact figures, testimonials or superlatives. |

Adjust formality to the actual channel and audience. Preserve names, dates, claims and qualifications supplied by the user. Flag contradictions instead of silently fixing substantive facts. Label illustrative content and unverified placeholders in drafts.

## Composition

**Skill defaults:** Establish one clear reading order: purpose, essential evidence or details, next action. Use scale, spacing and alignment to make that order visible. A spare event page, editorial research overview, or activity-led student homepage can all be Duke-branded. Avoid enforcing identical hero sections, card grids, color percentages or decorative motifs across them.

Use an official type pairing and one Duke blue as the identity anchor. Choose extended colors for a purpose, such as a section distinction or action, and verify each pairing. Let images support the subject rather than compensate for missing information. These are design choices, not additional university rules. For marks, the stricter [mark rules](marks.md) govern even where the identity page describes flexibility.
