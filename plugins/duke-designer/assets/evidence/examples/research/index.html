01 / The purpose

Study the organization, not just the algorithm.

Imagine a manager using an AI assistant to allocate work, a product team deciding when to release a new feature, or an employee questioning a recommendation. Each situation raises questions that a model’s performance score alone cannot answer.

This fictional initiative proposes studying the organizational conditions around those decisions: how responsibility is assigned, how people exercise judgment, and how affected groups can make their voices heard. Its focus would be the relationship between technical design and the everyday routines that shape its use.

The aim would be to develop useful questions and testable approaches for researchers, practitioners and communities. The proposals below describe what could be studied—not evidence of what works.

02 / Research themes

Three lenses on responsibility.

01

Accountability in practice

When a recommendation travels from a model to a team to a decision, responsibility can become difficult to trace. How might organizations make decision rights, review duties and routes for challenge visible?

A question to test: who has the authority to pause an AI-assisted decision?

02

Human judgment at work

Using AI changes the information people encounter and the time they have to act. What conditions would help employees examine a recommendation, recognize uncertainty and bring their own experience to a decision?

A question to test: when does an explanation support useful disagreement?

03

Participation by design

People affected by a system may have different priorities from the team building it. How could their perspectives shape the definition of a problem, the choice of measures and the decision to continue or stop?

A question to test: whose experience is missing from the evaluation?

Proposal 01 / Accountability

Mapping responsibility across an AI decision

A study of how decision ownership could be documented as an AI recommendation moves through a team’s workflow.

Read study outline +

Research question

Where do people disagree about who can approve, override or appeal a recommendation?

Proposed method

Use fictional workplace scenarios in facilitated workshops. Ask participants to map responsibilities independently, then compare the maps and discuss ambiguous handoffs.

Possible output & limits

A draft decision-mapping worksheet. Workshop responses would reflect a simulated setting; they would not establish how responsibility is exercised in an actual organization.

Proposal 02 / Human judgment

Making room for productive disagreement

A proposed comparison of how teams respond to AI advice when uncertainty and alternative interpretations are made visible.

Read study outline +

Research question

How might the presentation of uncertainty influence whether someone questions an AI recommendation?

Proposed method

Present participants with simulated planning tasks and alternative interface designs. Compare their reasoning, questions and willingness to seek additional information.

Possible output & limits

A set of candidate design principles for further testing. Task performance in a simulation would not establish effectiveness across workplaces or high-stakes decisions.

Proposal 03 / Participation

Including affected voices before deployment

An exploration of how a team could involve people affected by an AI system while it is still defining success.

Read study outline +

Research question

Which evaluation priorities emerge when affected groups help frame a proposed system’s purpose?

Proposed method

Invite participants to discuss fictional service scenarios, identify possible benefits and burdens, and co-design an evaluation checklist. Compare priorities without treating consensus as the only useful outcome.

Possible output & limits

A prototype workshop guide. Any later study would need a transparent recruitment strategy and attention to power differences; a workshop cannot represent every affected person.

Proposal 04 / Accountability

Learning from near misses without losing the context

A proposal for examining how teams could record, discuss and learn from AI-related concerns before harm occurs.

Read study outline +

Research question

What information would a review process need to understand both a system’s behavior and the circumstances surrounding its use?

Proposed method

Develop fictional incident vignettes and compare two ways of documenting them. Ask participants what each record makes visible, what it leaves out and what follow-up it suggests.

Possible output & limits

A draft learning-review template. Its value would require further evaluation; collecting more detail also creates privacy, access and retention questions.

04 / A research approach

Make the assumptions visible.

A responsible research agenda would need to examine its own choices as carefully as the systems it studies. These proposed commitments describe how future work could be planned and discussed.

Begin with a clear question.
Distinguish exploratory work from evaluation. Define the context, the people affected and what a study could reasonably establish.
Build in participant agency.
Plan for informed consent, accessible participation and appropriate review before any real study. Consider whose perspective the method may exclude.
Share uncertainty alongside the output.
Explain limitations, document changes in the research design and separate observed evidence from recommendations.

A starting point for discussion

What would you need to know before using AI in your organization?

Use the proposed studies as conversation starters. This demonstration does not recruit participants, collect personal information or offer research advice.
