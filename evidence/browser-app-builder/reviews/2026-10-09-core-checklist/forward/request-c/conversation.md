# Request C — simulated planning conversation

This is a **simulated** conversation for a forward exercise on 2026-10-09. Replies and numbers are invented teaching assumptions, not actual directors, market evidence or approval. No app was built.

**Opening request:** “I need an interactive board presentation recommending a new product launch. Show launch economics and market scenarios, include multiple useful charts and an appendix with the model, and let directors change assumptions during discussion. Public synthetic assumptions only.”

**Planner:** What decision should directors authorize, over what horizon, and what alternatives should the recommendation compare? Does changing assumptions mean local what-if edits or fresh external market data?

**Simulated student:** A fictional commercial product launch, with a three-year view. Compare full launch, limited launch and deferral. Directors should approve a scoped investment, not just discuss a classroom sale. Local what-if edits are enough; every market assumption must say synthetic.

**Planner:** May we use price $120/unit and variable cost $70, full-launch upfront cost $1.8m and annual fixed cost $750k, with 70,000 annual unit capacity? A limited launch costs $900k upfront and $450k/year, with 20,000 first-year and 30,000 later-year capacity. It stays limited rather than secretly gaining an expansion option. Deferral has zero modeled cash flows.

**Simulated student:** Yes. Use those as editable synthetic defaults. Exclude taxes, working capital, financing, terminal value and strategic value of waiting; state those limits in the appendix and near the recommendation. We will revisit them before a real decision.

**Planner:** Suggested synthetic scenarios are downside/base/upside with 25%/50%/25% weights, first-year demand 24k/40k/60k units and annual growth 0%/10%/15%. Use a 10% discount rate. Should we recommend the highest weighted NPV while requiring worst-scenario NPV of at least −$1m, and treat these weights as assumptions rather than measured probabilities?

**Simulated student:** Yes. That makes the decision rule explicit. Make the downside floor editable. Use one demand multiplier for discussion plus editable scenario inputs. Do not quietly normalize weights that do not add to 100%.

**Planner:** Independently calculated first load favors full launch: weighted NPV about $1.860m versus $1.138m limited; full launch’s downside is −$0.681m and passes the floor. Reducing the demand multiplier to 70% switches to limited launch, about $0.960m weighted NPV; full launch then has about $0.264m expected NPV and −$1.576m downside. A proposal slide can therefore change its recommendation with the evidence.

**Simulated student:** Agreed. Ask directors to approve the selected launch’s upfront investment and annual fixed budget. Show a scenario comparison, an economics breakdown and capacity/demand over time; those answer different questions. Keep the model appendix one click away and return to the same slide.

**Planner:** Use live valid-input updates, with incomplete fields and stale last-valid results clearly marked. Provide a visible presentation/full-screen control, exit and browser-denied-fullscreen fallback, and keep input arrow keys separate from slide navigation. Phones get readable stacked slides and charts rather than a shrunken presentation.

**Simulated student:** Yes. Preserve all those requirements. A short main story with an accessible appendix works; no external presenter service or media is needed. This plan is agreed for the simulation, with no material questions left.
