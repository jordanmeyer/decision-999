# Imagery

**Duke recommendation — [S7 Imagery](sources.md):** portray an energetic, varied and ambitious community. The public identity examples show people researching, learning, socializing, serving the community and engaging internationally, alongside campus architecture. They illustrate subject range, not a mandatory subject list or permission to reuse the photographs.

The linked photography mood board is an inspiration resource, not a stock-photo license. It redirected to Duke sign-in and was **not inspected**. Duke's Asset Management System at [duke.webdamdb.com/cloud/#landing](https://duke.webdamdb.com/cloud/#landing) is explicitly NetID-required. The former public “Photography at Duke” link now redirects to the Communications homepage; its current [Multimedia Guide](https://communications.duke.edu/communicator-resources/guides/multimedia-guide/) also requires sign-in. Do not invent cropping, retouching, release, or filter rules from these unread resources.

## Apply available evidence

**Skill defaults:** Use a relevant authorized photograph when it advances the story; a text-led design is also valid. Preserve meaningful subjects and context when cropping, and check each responsive crop. Keep text on a separate plain panel when an image cannot support reliable contrast. Avoid embellishments that misrepresent people, places or research; these are implementation choices, not newly asserted Duke policies. The [mark background restrictions](marks.md) still apply.

Record source, permission/usage constraints and required credit for every production image. Public visibility and a screenshot of an example do not establish reuse rights. Do not present synthetic campus imagery or invented people as documentary evidence. Label demonstration imagery and data clearly.

If the library is inaccessible, use an authorized user-supplied image, an explicit draft placeholder, or a composition without a photograph. Continue independent design work. If the task specifically requires a restricted asset or its unavailable usage conditions, identify that exact dependency and defer the affected placement. Do not ask for credentials.

**General accessibility practice:** Provide meaningful alternatives for informative images and empty alternatives for purely decorative web images; do not repeat nearby captions unnecessarily. Complex charts need an equivalent explanation/data. Captions and credit remain distinct from alternative text. Verify the actual crop and the intended reading order in the rendered artifact.
